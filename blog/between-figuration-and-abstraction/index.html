Between Figuration and Abstraction: Finding the Human Form in Chaos

Publication pending

There is a misconception that abstract painting abandons the human figure. That in the move toward color and form, the body is lost. I do not believe this. I believe the body goes underground. It becomes the archaeology of the painting. You do not see it. You sense its weight.

The Trace of a Shoulder

In the Emotional Big Bang series, what first appears as pure abstraction reveals, on longer looking, the ghost of a shoulder. The curve of a spine. The negative space where a head once turned. These are not deliberate representations. They are residues. The canvas remembers the body even when the artist has moved on.

This is the difference between figuration and abstraction. Figuration says: Here is a body. Abstraction says: Here is what remains after the body has left.

Why the Figure Hides

I do not set out to paint a figure. But the figure insists. It emerges in the second layer, or the fifth, when the conscious mind is tired and the hand moves on its own. This is muscle memory at work — not the memory of the eye, but of the arm, the wrist, the fingers that have held brushes for decades. The body knows how to draw a back before the mind decides whether to allow it.

What Collectors Discover

Long looking can reveal something not noticed at first: a knee, a collarbone, or a hand reaching from the lower edge. The painting has not changed. Their looking has deepened. This is the gift of abstraction: it does not give everything at once. It waits.

The Space Between

If you are drawn to work that exists between the recognizable and the unknown, you are looking for painting that respects your intelligence. It does not explain. It invites. The figure is there if you need it. And if you do not, the color and the gesture are enough.

Works from the Emotional Big Bang series are available for direct inquiry. If you are interested in the dialogue between figuration and abstraction, or if you would like to see how these pieces behave in person, please reach out.
