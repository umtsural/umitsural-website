The Red Series: Why I Keep Returning to the Color of Memory

Publication pending

I have tried to leave red behind. I have opened tubes of ochre, mixed quiet grays, promised myself a season of restraint. But red returns. Not because it is loud. Because it is honest. In my work, red is not a choice. It is a confession.

The Heat of Unfinished Movement

In the Muscle Memory series, red appears where the body remembers what the mind has edited out. A hand raised too quickly. A step that landed wrong. A silence that lasted too long. These moments do not have shapes. They have temperatures. And red, in this context, is not hot. It is held. It is the color of a memory that has not been allowed to cool.

The red does not need to attack; it can remain as a sustained presence. It occupies the room like a guest who has nowhere else to be. This is the red I am interested in. Not the red of warning, but the red of witness.

Why Red Survives Editing

Every painting goes through a phase where it could become something else. The palette shifts. The forms soften. But red, once introduced, resists dilution. It does not blend into the background. It negotiates. It demands a border, a relationship, a reason to be there. This stubbornness is what makes it a reliable narrator. It will not lie about its own presence.

The Collector’s Relationship

A red painting may seem to change with the viewer’s own emotional temperature. On calm days, it recedes. On difficult days, it advances. It becomes a kind of barometer. Not because the pigment moves, but because the viewer’s capacity to hold red shifts. The painting is constant. The relationship is not.

For Those Drawn to Red

If you are considering a work with red, do not ask whether it matches your room. Ask whether you are ready to live with a color that will not apologize. Red is not a neutral choice. It is a commitment to presence.

A selection of red works from the Muscle Memory series may be available through the studio. If you are drawn to this palette and would like to discuss scale, placement, or the specific history of a piece, I am available directly.
