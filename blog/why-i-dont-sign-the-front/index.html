Why I Don’t Sign the Front of My Paintings

Publication pending

Every painting I finish presents me with the same choice. A corner, usually the lower right, waits empty. The brush is loaded with a dark color. One gesture, two seconds, and the work is marked. Mine. Claimed. Branded with the evidence of a hand that will eventually die.

I do not make that gesture. The signature goes on the back.

The Front Is for the Work

The front of a painting is a stage. It is the only place where the work can speak without interruption. When a signature sits in the corner, it acts as a caption. It says: Before you look, know who made this. It frames the experience before the experience has begun. I am not interested in that frame.

I want the viewer to encounter the surface without preamble. To feel something before they know my name. If the painting cannot hold attention without a signature, the signature is a bribe. And I do not make work that needs to be bribed.

The Back Is for the Record

The back of the canvas is where the facts live. The date. The materials. The title. My name, written small, in a place only the installer and the conservator will see. This is not modesty. It is architecture. The front is the room. The back is the foundation. Both are necessary. But only one is meant to be inhabited.

What Collectors Say

Turning a piece over may reveal that the signature has been kept away from the front surface. They assume it is a conceptual gesture. It is not. It is a practical one. The painting belongs to the room it hangs in, not to the person who made it. My name on the back is a record of origin. The work on the front is a record of presence. Presence is more important than origin.

The Conversation

A painting without a front signature invites a different kind of looking. The viewer cannot rely on reputation to validate their response. They must trust their own eyes. Their own hesitation. Their own decision to stay or move on. This is the conversation I am interested in: not between artist and market, but between surface and stranger.

For Those Who Collect

If you own or are considering a work without a front signature, you are participating in this silence. The painting does not announce its maker. It announces itself. And in doing so, it leaves room for you.

All works are signed, titled, and dated on the reverse. Provenance and authentication documentation can be discussed for individual works. If you have questions about a specific work’s history or condition, I respond directly.
